Mission statement

Build something you'd defend in production

The capstone isn't a hackathon toy. It's a miniature production system that uses at least one classical ML component (Days 1–2), at least one generative or agentic component (Days 3–4), and the engineering discipline of Day 5: evaluation, safety and cost.

You'll reuse what you already built. The churn model, the pipeline, the briefing engine and the concierge agent are all fair game, so the sprint goes to integration and proof instead of starting from scratch.

  • Small and real beats big and imaginary. One working path end-to-end wins.
  • Show your numbers. Eval scores, attack success rates and cost per request.
  • Tell a story. Who's the user, what hurts, and what changes on Monday?

All crews, this is Mission Control. You've crossed five worlds this week, and every artifact you built is cargo you can load onto this rocket. The fleet board will be in the room at 16:00. They have read none of your code and all of your hype. Show them a system that works, numbers that prove it, and a plan for when it breaks. Launch window opens at 13:45. Good luck. Mission Control out.

🏁 The Launch

Six deliverables stand between your crew and orbit. Check each one off as you complete it. The HP bar is your launch countdown, and all six together earn +500 XP and the Launched badge.

Launch criteria

  • Architecture diagram: one slide showing data, classical model(s), Gemini/agents, tools, guard layer and feedback loop (start from Fig C.1).
  • Working demo: a live (or recorded backup) end-to-end path on real Google Cloud resources, with no mocked model calls.
  • Eval scorecard: at least 10 test cases scored with at least one autorater or rubric metric, plus one deterministic metric, compared against a baseline.
  • Cost model: cost per request (or per 1,000 users) with the model mix you chose, and one optimization you applied (cascade, caching or batch).
  • Red-team report: the before/after attack table from your Day 5 boss battle, with residual risks you accept and why.
  • 3-minute pitch: problem → demo → numbers → what breaks next. Practiced at least once with a timer.
🎤
SIDE QUESTPitch in 60 Seconds

Before the real pitch, deliver the whole story (problem, demo moment, one number, the risk) to another crew in 60 seconds flat. If they can repeat your value proposition back, claim it.

🐒
SIDE QUEST · LIVEChaos Monkey

During your demo, deliberately break something: kill a tool, feed a poisoned document, or exhaust a quota. Then show the system degrading gracefully. Fleet boards love a crew that rehearsed failure.
